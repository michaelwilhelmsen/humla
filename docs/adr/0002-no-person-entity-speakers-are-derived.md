# No Person entity — a Speaker is derived from transcripts, never a stored record

Three in-flight features were each reaching for "a person" and each solving it their own way: #104 derives a note's speakers from transcript labels, #116 wants autocomplete that joins the same person across notes, and #115 pins a conversation to a Client whose recurring speakers are that account's contacts. None of them needs a person table; all three would be *slightly* better with one; and any of them could accidentally become one — a `speaker_labels` cache is one schema change away from being a contacts table nobody designed. We decided **Humla has no Person/Contact entity, deliberately**: a person is a query dimension, never a destination and never a record.

The boundary runs on **consent**, not on storage mechanics. Humla may derive freely from what a person opted into — the workspace roster, where members gave us a name and an email — and from transcript text the user wrote or corrected. It must never create a new store of claims about someone who did not opt in. A speaker is usually the customer, not a member: Hege is K2's, not yours. For an EU-hosted product whose pitch is privacy and local ownership, "we keep a contact record on people who attended your meetings" reads very differently from "we keep a transcript", even when the bytes are identical.

The mechanical test that follows: **if it cannot be rebuilt from transcripts plus the roster, it is a record rather than a cache, and it does not get built.** Identity is therefore asserted in exactly one place — the transcript text, via the speaker rename. There is no alias table, no merge table, and no person→Client link. The only way to fix who someone is, is to rewrite the transcripts that name them.

## Considered Options

- **A Person/Contact entity with an alias/merge map.** Rejected: it is the only option that genuinely beats a string — one action fixes four spellings, and two people sharing a label get distinct ids — but it buys that by storing user-asserted claims about non-consenting third parties, and it needs a sync story, a merge story, and a GDPR-erasure story for a non-user, none of which any current entity needs. The cost is real and the benefit is narrow.
- **A minimal Person entity with no aliases.** Rejected as buying nothing. Without an alias map it is the distinct-label list with a uuid attached, and the uuid is useless at retrieval time: retrieval is BM25 plus embeddings over **text**, so the uuid is not in the index. Filtering by it would need a `note_speakers(note_id, person_id)` join table populated by matching label strings — the same string matching the entity was meant to replace, relocated to write time. #116's autocomplete already relocates it to write time. Two mechanisms, one job.
- **No blanket rule; let each feature justify its own storage.** Rejected: that is precisely the process that produces an accidental contacts table, and is why this decision was taken separately rather than inside any one feature.
- **No entity; identity asserted only in the transcript (chosen).**

## Consequences

- **#104's open question is answered "no".** We do not need a workspace-level identity map. The rename is a good enough join key — *conditionally*, which is what makes #116 load-bearing rather than a nicety: prevention is the only repair that scales, so the autocomplete is the cross-note identity strategy, not a convenience.
- **Repair is a cross-note rename, and that primitive is now required.** #116's part 2 is rescoped from "bulk-fix `You`" to "rename this speaker everywhere", with `You` as its first application rather than its purpose. Without it, converging four spellings means finding and opening four notes, and the practical objection to derived-only identity stands. With it, convergence is one action that still rewrites the source of truth, so the transcript never disagrees with what the user reads.
  - **Amended (as built): the primitive collapses "every note carrying this label", not "every spelling of this person".** The sentence above was written assuming part 2 would include a surface listing which labels exist. It doesn't — a permanent speaker-management view was rejected as dead weight at a few dozen notes, so the entry points are the chip strip inside a note and one count-gated Settings row for the `You` case. Converging four spellings therefore means opening **one note per spelling**, not every note carrying it. The residual cost is *discovery*: nothing in the app tells you that four spellings exist. Accepted, on the grounds that prevention (#116 part 1's picker) is what scales and the legacy mess is finite and small. Recorded here rather than left as a consequence that overstates what was built.
- **Derived speaker data is destroyed with its source, not merely hidden.** Any derived person data must be dropped when its note is, and any rebuild reads live notes only. This is the rule's only teeth in code, and it makes "delete the note" a real answer to erasure. The cloud indexer already complies (`applyNote` calls `store.removeNote` on a tombstone); local `notes_purge` does not clear `note_chunks` / `note_chunks_fts`, which is a pre-existing leak filed separately.
- **Erasure of a non-user is "delete or edit the notes naming them".** There is no *forget this person* action, because there is no person record to forget. This is a defensible answer precisely because the derived data cannot outlive the transcript.
- **Querying and `@`-mentions work on strings.** `notes.speakers` gives a note-level filter, `note_chunks.speakers` a chunk-level one, and the `speaker` tool argument matches exactly and case-insensitively. An `@Hege` mention in the chat composer resolves to `speaker: "Hege Tronshaugen"` — a string filter over columns #104 builds. "Humla has no person entity" is **not** a blocker for that feature and should not be cited as one.
- **A cross-note rename is only sound for a *name*, never for a per-recording label.** `You` means whoever held the mic and `Speaker 1` means whoever the diarizer clustered first, so each denotes a **different person in every recording**. Sweeping one across notes therefore writes false attribution rather than converging an identity — in a workspace, into a teammate's meeting. This was found in use: #116 part 2 initially took the issue's "the sweep includes teammates' notes, deliberately" at face value, and renaming `You` → "Michael" rewrote the speaker in a colleague's recording to Michael, where `You` had been the colleague. That decision is right for a name (Hege is the same Hege in everyone's notes, which is what makes converging her four spellings a repair) and wrong for an indexical. Both rules now hold in code: the chip strip offers no sweep at all for a per-recording label, and the `You` action in Settings is scoped to notes the user recorded — the one scope in which `You` resolves to them.
- **Known cost: two different people may share one label.** "Michael" can be both the user and Michael at K2, and no string filter separates them where an id could. #104 chose exact matching so two *spellings* don't merge into a confident wrong answer, but exact matching cannot help when the label itself is identical. The repair is renaming one of them to something distinct. This is permanent, and accepted.
- **The roster stays a legitimate input.** #116 may suggest workspace-member names before the user has typed them, and #104 may resolve `You:` via the note's owner. Both derive from a consented store and are recomputed rather than stored as claims about a speaker.
  - **Amended (#116 part 1): the roster may also be cached verbatim.** The picker persists the active workspace's `CloudMember` records in a `roster_cache:<workspace_id>` settings row, refreshed on every successful member fetch and deleted on leave-workspace, delete-workspace and logout. Best-effort-fetch-only was rejected because a cold launch while offline is exactly when you are labelling a recording you just made. This narrows the "recomputed rather than stored" wording above rather than opening a new question: the cache mirrors a store the workspace already holds, whose members consented to being named, it holds no user-asserted claim about who a speaker *is*, and because it dies with the membership it still cannot outlive its source.
- **`speaker_labels` was permitted and then not built.** The suggestion source is a plain scan of `notes.speakers` (`db::speaker_label_stats`), because `notes.speakers` is delimiter-wrapped and cannot be indexed by substring anyway, and at a few thousand notes the scan is instant. The derived table stays the optimization to add when it stops being — under the same two constraints, if it ever lands: no field that isn't derivable, and rows die with the notes that produced them.
- **This does not reopen ADR-0001.** That decision rejected fuzzy-matching Clients from participants partly because "no structured participant-tracking exists today", and #104 creates exactly that. Manual-only Client tagging still stands — it rests on reliability (a wrong silent auto-tag corrupts that Client's Rollup), not on the absence of the data.
- **Vocabulary is fixed on Speaker** (see `CONTEXT.md`), so #104's local column is `notes.speakers`, matching `note_chunks.speakers` and the `speaker` tool argument. "Participant" and "attendee" are avoided as overclaims: diarization only ever knows who *spoke*, and a silent attendee produces no label.
